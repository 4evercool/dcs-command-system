## Execution mode

**Sequential: S1-CONTRACT → S2-GUARD → S3-RECORD → S4-RECONCILE.** Not a
performance compromise — the ordering is the fix.

- **S2 after S1** because S2's population, citation form and live step
  number all come from S1's finished prose. Running them together would
  have S2 writing a comparator against text that is still moving, and *"two
  artefacts built against different snapshots of one contract"* is the
  exact failure this incident is at halt 2 over.
- **S3 after both** because it describes what they actually landed, and its
  surface list is rebuilt from `git diff --stat` at end state.
- **S4 last**, read-only, before Safety.

Territories are pairwise disjoint by inspection — the IC verified this
rather than accepting `partition_ok: true`.

## Why the roster changed from revision 1

**Revision 1 split the contract surfaces across two specialists, and the
halt landed exactly on that seam.** S3 widened `REGISTER.md`'s `DEPLOYED`
definition and missed the facts-only block thirteen lines below it, while
S2's `deploy.md:121` cited the un-widened rule as authority. Re-splitting
the same surfaces would recreate the same seam, so **one hand now owns all
six declaring files**.

The Planning Chief spent three of the permitted four deliberately, on the
grounds that the only credible fourth was a second prose owner — which is
the measured cause of halt 2. **The IC used the fourth slot anyway, for
the one thing that cannot recreate the seam:** a read-only reconciliation
pass that performs the Safety Officer's own manual read *before* Safety
sees it. Its findings route as fix-taskings to the owning specialist; it
edits nothing.
