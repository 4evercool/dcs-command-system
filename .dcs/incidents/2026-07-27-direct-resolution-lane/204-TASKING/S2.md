## Check-15 hazard in your territory too — read this before rewording anything

`new.md`, `forms.md` and `close.md` are all inside check 15's walked population
(`*.md` under `dcs/`). A paragraph becomes **declaring** iff it has all three of:
the literal token `DEPLOYED`; a proof word
(`verify|verifies|verified|read|confirm|confirms|confirmed|proof|ancestor|ancestors|witness|witnesses|evidence`);
and a rule shape (``DEPLOYED`?\s*(--|:)`` · `DEPLOYED only after|once|when|if` ·
the Unicode arrow `→` U+2192 immediately before `DEPLOYED`). A new declaring file
adds a Rule A check — moving the `82` total — and demands a `deploy.md` step 7
citation that has no business being in `close.md`.

**The generic advice "just don't use the token `DEPLOYED`" does not work for you**,
because `close.md:239` already contains it and you may have to reword that very
sentence. The operative rule is the predicate itself:

> `close.md`'s `:238-246` paragraph already contains `DEPLOYED` **and** Unicode
> arrows, and is non-declaring **only because it has no proof word.** That
> absence is its entire margin. Your reword must **add none of those proof
> words to that paragraph**, and must never place `→` immediately before
> `DEPLOYED`.

In `new.md` and `forms.md`, where you are adding rather than rewording, the cheap
rule does hold: do not use the token `DEPLOYED` in any new sentence.
